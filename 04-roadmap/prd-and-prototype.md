# PRD & Prototype Sprint (Module 4)

## Pick & scope with MoSCoW
- **The “Now” feature I’m scoping (name + one-line core description):** Spotlight Curated Rail-A hand-picked homepage rail that bypasses the algorithm entirely, surfacing 3 to 5 human-curated titles the moment a Wanderer lands on the homepage, intercepting the confirmed 29-point Homepage-to-Browse drop-off before any scrolling or decision-making is required.
- **My finalized Must-Haves (after overriding the AI):** Must Have scope for this sprint centers on rendering the curated rail directly on the homepage, above the fold and built mobile-first, displaying a minimum of three to five curated titles to ensure the experience feels genuinely curated rather than sparse. Curation itself will be handled through direct data entry rather than a dedicated content-team-facing tool, keeping engineering effort focused on the rail experience rather than internal tooling this sprint. The team will track impressions and clicks on the rail to measure movement against the primary metric, and will protect the guardrail through a manual, weekly check against existing Power User curated engagement reporting, an interim, non-automated approach that avoids building new monitoring infrastructure before the core hypothesis has been validated.
- **What I demoted from Must → Should/Won’t, and why:** Two Must-Have items, the curation tool and automated guardrail monitoring, were demoted to Should Have because they were actually separate engineering projects, not simple sub-tasks. Both were replaced with lean, manual interim versions, preserving the guardrail and core functionality while keeping the sprint realistically shippable in three weeks with two engineers.

## Generate your Simplified PRD
- **One thing my PRD makes explicit that a vague brief would have missed:** Honestly, the thing that stands out most is the decision to skip building a curation tool for the content team and just use manual data entry instead. If the brief had just said "let the content team manage the curated titles," it's easy to imagine engineers quietly assuming that meant building a whole interface, and losing days of a three week sprint before anyone even noticed. Writing out the reasoning behind that call keeps everyone focused on what we're actually trying to prove, that curation reduces churn for Wanderers, instead of accidentally spending the sprint building tooling that could easily wait for later.
