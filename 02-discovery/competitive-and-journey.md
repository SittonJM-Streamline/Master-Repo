# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** The Quality-Sensitive Subscriber
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Feel confident a recommendation won't be a waste of 20 minutes before giving up on it
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** No sense of why something is recommended, which erodes trust over time
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** StreamLineTV and general film subreddits; Letterboxd (to check ratings/reviews from people with similar taste); Rotten Tomatoes/Metacritic in a second browser tab; occasionally a group chat text to a friend (“worth it?”)
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** 1. Sees a title StreamLine recommends. 2. Opens a new tab and searches “[title] reddit worth watching” or checks Letterboxd rating. 3. Skims 2–3 comments/reviews for a specific reason it’s good or bad  not just a star rating. 4. Cross-checks against Rotten Tomatoes if reviews are mixed. 5. Only then returns to StreamLine to actually press play or abandons the title entirely.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The moment they realize they’re doing StreamLine’s job for it that the platform surfaced the title but gave them zero reasoning, so they have to go find the reasoning themselves before they’ll trust it with their time.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** Directly from the Problem Hook: subscribers are “losing” trust “one quiet Reddit search at a time” the behavior itself (leaving the app to validate a recommendation) is the evidence. It shows the 15,000-title catalog isn’t the value prop; curation with a visible reason is, and its absence is what sends people to Reddit instead.
